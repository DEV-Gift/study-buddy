Quick Lesson Notes

Start with these important Chemistry concepts.

1. Atoms and Elements

An atom is the basic unit of a chemical element. It contains a nucleus made up of protons and usually neutrons, with electrons around the nucleus.

An element is a pure substance made up of atoms with the same number of protons. Examples include oxygen, hydrogen, and carbon.

2. Acids and Bases

Acids and bases have different chemical properties. In aqueous solution, acids increase the concentration of hydrogen ions, while bases can neutralise acids.

The pH scale is commonly used to describe how acidic or basic an aqueous solution is. A pH below 7 is acidic, 7 is neutral, and above 7 is basic at approximately 25°C.

3. Chemical Reactions

A chemical reaction changes starting substances, called reactants, into new substances called products.

For example, hydrogen reacts with oxygen to form water. Chemical equations represent these changes using chemical symbols and formulas.

4. States of Matter

The three familiar states of matter are solids, liquids, and gases. Solids have a definite shape and volume, liquids have a definite volume but take the shape of their container, and gases expand to fill their container.

Changes in temperature and pressure can cause matter to change state. Examples include melting, freezing, evaporation, and condensation.
